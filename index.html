WWI Study Guide

Review all the major terms and topics from your examination study guide. Definitions are written in their World War I context.

Vocabulary
Alliance

An agreement between countries to support and defend each other. European alliances helped turn a regional conflict into a larger war.

Militarism

The buildup of military forces and weapons. European countries expanded their armies and navies before WWI.

Nationalism

Strong pride and loyalty toward one's nation or ethnic group. Nationalism increased rivalries and tensions in Europe.

Imperialism

When a powerful country extends control over other lands and people. Competition for colonies increased tensions before WWI.

U-Boat

A German submarine used to attack enemy and merchant ships during WWI.

Trench Warfare

A style of fighting where soldiers fought from long defensive trenches. It made battles difficult and often resulted in little movement.

Stalemate

A situation where neither side can make decisive progress or defeat the other.

No Man's Land

The dangerous, exposed area between opposing trenches.

Dogfight

An aerial battle between fighter airplanes.

Lusitania

A British passenger ship sunk by a German U-Boat in May 1915. Americans were among those killed, increasing anger toward Germany.

Neutrality

The policy of not officially taking sides in a war. The United States remained neutral until 1917.

Zimmermann Telegram

A secret German message proposing an alliance with Mexico if the United States entered the war. Germany suggested Mexico could try to regain territory lost to the United States.

Four Minute Men

Volunteers who gave short patriotic speeches to encourage Americans to support the war effort.

Food Administration

A U.S. agency led by Herbert Hoover that encouraged food conservation so more supplies could go to soldiers and Allied countries.

Propaganda

Messages, images, or information designed to influence people's opinions and encourage support for a cause or government.

Selective Service Act

A 1917 law requiring eligible American men to register for the military draft.

Committee on Public Information

A U.S. government agency that used posters, speeches, films, and publications to build public support for WWI.

War Industries Board

A U.S. agency that coordinated industrial production and directed materials toward wartime needs.

Abdicate

To give up a throne or position as ruler. Czar Nicholas II abdicated in 1917.

Armistice

An agreement to stop fighting. The WWI armistice took effect on November 11, 1918.

Fourteen Points

Woodrow Wilson's 1918 peace plan. It included open diplomacy, reduced weapons, self-determination, and a League of Nations.

Big Four

The four major Allied leaders at the Paris Peace Conference: Woodrow Wilson, David Lloyd George, Georges Clemenceau, and Vittorio Orlando.

Self-Determination

The idea that people should have the right to choose their own government and political future.

Reparations

Payments made by a country to compensate for damage caused by war. Germany was required to pay reparations after WWI.

Important People
Franz Ferdinand

Heir to the Austria-Hungary throne. His assassination in 1914 triggered the crisis that led to WWI.

Woodrow Wilson

U.S. president during WWI. He asked Congress to declare war in 1917 and proposed the Fourteen Points.

Herbert Hoover

Led the Food Administration and encouraged Americans to conserve food.

Czar Nicholas II

Russia's ruler who abdicated during the Russian Revolution in 1917.

Vladimir Lenin

Bolshevik leader who took power in Russia in 1917. His government later took Russia out of WWI.

Kaiser Wilhelm II

Germany's emperor during most of WWI. He abdicated in 1918.

John J. Pershing

U.S. general who commanded the American Expeditionary Forces in Europe.

Six Propaganda Techniques
1. Testimonial

Using a famous or respected person to support an idea or product.

2. Name-calling

Using insulting labels to make an opponent look bad.

3. Bandwagon

Making people believe everyone is doing something, so they should join too.

4. Fear

Using a threat or frightening possibility to persuade people.

5. Card-stacking

Showing only selected facts that support one side while leaving out opposing information.

6. Word Magic

Using emotional or powerful words to make an idea sound appealing.
