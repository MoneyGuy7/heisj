WWI Vocabulary

These definitions explain what each term means specifically in the context of World War I.

AllianceAn agreement between countries to support and defend each other. Alliances helped turn the conflict in 1914 into a larger European war.
MilitarismThe buildup of a country's military forces and weapons. European nations expanded their armies and navies before WWI.
NationalismStrong pride and loyalty toward one's nation or ethnic group. Nationalism increased rivalries and tensions in Europe.
ImperialismWhen a powerful country extends its control over other lands and people. Competition for colonies increased tensions before WWI.
U-BoatA German submarine used to attack enemy and merchant ships during WWI.
Trench WarfareA style of fighting where soldiers fought from long, deep trenches. It made battles difficult and often caused very little movement.
StalemateA situation where neither side can make enough progress to defeat the other. Trench warfare created a stalemate on the Western Front.
No Man's LandThe dangerous, exposed area between opposing trenches.
DogfightAn aerial battle between fighter airplanes.
LusitaniaA British passenger ship sunk by a German U-Boat in May 1915. Americans were among the civilians killed, increasing anger toward Germany.
NeutralityThe policy of not taking sides in a war. The United States stayed officially neutral until 1917.
Zimmermann TelegramA secret German message proposing an alliance with Mexico if the United States entered WWI. Germany suggested Mexico could try to regain territory lost to the U.S.
Four Minute MenVolunteers who gave short patriotic speeches to encourage Americans to support the war effort.
Food AdministrationA U.S. agency led by Herbert Hoover that encouraged Americans to conserve food so more supplies could go to soldiers and Allied countries.
PropagandaInformation, posters, or messages designed to influence people's opinions and encourage support for a cause or government.
Selective Service ActA 1917 law requiring eligible American men to register for the military draft.
Committee on Public InformationA U.S. government agency that used posters, speeches, films, and publications to build public support for WWI.
War Industries BoardA U.S. agency that coordinated industrial production and directed materials toward wartime needs.
AbdicateTo give up a throne or position as ruler. Czar Nicholas II abdicated in 1917.
ArmisticeAn agreement to stop fighting. The WWI armistice took effect on November 11, 1918.
Fourteen PointsPresident Woodrow Wilson's 1918 peace plan. It included ideas such as self-determination, reduced weapons, and a League of Nations.
Big FourThe four major Allied leaders at the Paris Peace Conference: Woodrow Wilson, David Lloyd George, Georges Clemenceau, and Vittorio Orlando.
Self-DeterminationThe idea that people should have the right to choose their own government and political future.
ReparationsPayments made by a country to compensate for damage caused by war. Germany was required to pay reparations after WWI.

Important People

Franz FerdinandHeir to the Austria-Hungary throne. His assassination in 1914 triggered the crisis that led to WWI.
Woodrow WilsonU.S. president during WWI. He asked Congress to declare war in 1917 and proposed the Fourteen Points.
Herbert HooverLed the Food Administration and encouraged Americans to conserve food.
Czar Nicholas IIRussia's ruler who abdicated during the Russian Revolution in 1917.
Vladimir LeninBolshevik leader who took power in Russia in 1917. His government later took Russia out of WWI.
Kaiser Wilhelm IIGermany's emperor during most of WWI. He abdicated in 1918.
John J. PershingU.S. general who commanded the American Expeditionary Forces in Europe.

Allied and Central Powers

Allied PowersMajor Allied countries included Britain, France, Russia, and Italy. Italy joined the Allies in 1915, while Russia left the war in 1918.
Central PowersGermany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire, and Bulgaria.

Propaganda Techniques

TestimonialUsing a famous or respected person to support an idea.
Name-callingUsing insulting names or labels to make an opponent look bad.
BandwagonMaking people believe everyone is doing something, so they should join too.
FearUsing a threat or frightening possibility to persuade people.
Card-stackingShowing only selected facts that support one side while leaving out other information.
Word MagicUsing emotional or powerful words to make an idea sound appealing.

WWI Timeline

June 28, 1914Franz Ferdinand is assassinated.
July–August 1914Declarations of war spread the conflict across Europe.
August 1914Germany invades Belgium; Britain enters the war.
1915A German U-Boat sinks the Lusitania.
1917The Russian Revolution takes place. The Zimmermann Telegram is revealed. The United States enters WWI.
April 6, 1917The United States declares war on Germany.
March 1918The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk takes Russia out of the war.
November 11, 1918The armistice ends the fighting.
June 28, 1919The Treaty of Versailles is signed.
